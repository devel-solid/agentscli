# Replace review adjectives with usable criteria

## Hypothetical context

A fictional team has agreed to prefix review comments with `required` for a violation of an established agreement or acceptance criterion, `question` when information is missing to judge compliance, and `suggestion` for an optional alternative. A preference alone cannot make a comment required. Required comments identify the applicable agreement and the conflicting behavior. These are teaching assumptions, not agentscli policies.

Read the complete [inadequate convention](inadequate/review-comment-labels.md) and [corrected convention](corrected/review-comment-labels.md).

## What fails

The inadequate document sounds reasonable: consistent labels should help readers understand a review. But “important,” “unclear,” and “useful improvement” overlap. A reviewer can call a preferred variable name important and label it required without contradicting that text. The draft names the labels without recording the team's criterion for choosing them.

## The reader's unresolved decision

Is a different naming preference a required change, a question, or a suggestion? What evidence makes a comment required rather than optional?

## What changes and why

The correction ties each label to the state of the evidence. A compact comparison works because the reader chooses among three parallel alternatives. The following review comments keep the same subject while changing the decisive fact: a documented naming agreement exists, the reviewer does not know whether one exists, or the reviewer merely prefers a different name. The explanation makes the optionality visible instead of relying on the labels to explain themselves.

No review gate or new approval rule is added. The correction expresses the already established classification and the evidence required for a required comment.

## Transferable concept

An adjective describes a desired quality; an agreement gives a reader a criterion for choosing between plausible actions. Ask what fact would change the choice, then make that fact visible.
