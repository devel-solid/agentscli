# Queue ownership

Working from a shared queue requires care because several people may contribute to the same item and everyone needs a reliable understanding of what is happening. Ownership gives the team a clear point of contact and makes collaboration easier. Each active queue item therefore has exactly one named owner. This helps avoid uncertainty about responsibility and ensures that people know whom to ask when the work needs attention.

Keeping the status current is an important part of that responsibility. The named owner is responsible for maintaining the item's current status so that the queue remains useful to colleagues who were not involved in the latest conversation. Other people can help with the work, and their contributions are valuable, but helping does not make them additional owners. One owner remains the point of responsibility even when the work is collaborative.

Handoffs need particular attention because they create a period in which responsibility can become less obvious. When another person is proposed as the next owner, communicate that proposed transfer clearly. The current owner remains responsible until the recipient explicitly accepts ownership; only then does the owner change. This maintains continuity during the transfer and prevents an item from losing its owner while people assume that someone else has taken over. Treat that acceptance as the boundary rather than assuming that mentioning a colleague is enough to complete the handoff.

Applying these agreements consistently helps the team collaborate effectively and keeps the queue understandable as people join, leave, or help with a piece of work.
